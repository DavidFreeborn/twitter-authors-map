# Label Review Summary

> Labels describe prevalent post content. They do not apply to every post, classify every author, or establish authors' identities, occupations, locations, intentions or relationships.

## Omega-squared results in plain language

Across the top 21 communities (188,765 authors; 95.2% coverage), community membership was associated on average with:

- 21.1% of the variation in topic weights;
- 32.6% of the variation in sentiment probabilities; and
- 27.6% of the variation in emotion scores.

The strongest topic associations were Web3/DeFi 63.3%, AI Art Discourse 53.4%, NFT/Crypto 33.1%, General AI 29.6%, AI Tools/Code 21.4%. The strongest emotion associations were trust 44.3%, disgust 41.7%, anger 39.7%, optimism 38.2%, joy 36.1%.

Dominant topic and community membership had Cramer's V = 0.330. A 15-community sensitivity check produced very similar average omega-squared values (topics 21.0%, sentiment 33.4%, emotions 28.2%), so the aggregate association pattern is not driven only by the six smallest displayed communities. These are associations, not causal effects, and they do not mean that every author or post in a community shares the same attributes.

## Methodological limitations

- The labels summarise prevalent post content and are not author-level identity, occupation, geography, intention or behavioural classifications.
- The focused audit inspected a reproducible sample of 25 posts from 25 distinct authors in each of 14 inferentially sensitive communities (350 posts total). One post per author reduces prolific-account dominance but cannot capture all within-community variation.
- C3, C13, C14, C15 and C28 are explicitly low-confidence provisional labels.
- Discussion about a place, regionally associated wording and author location are different claims. The C13/C14 audit found no explicit self-reported location in the sampled posts; location was not established.
- In C14, 208 of 26,069 non-empty posts (0.8%) contained Air India. A separate 25-post check across five authors found airline, flight or aviation content in all 25. Air India therefore represents a confirmed false-positive route into the AI corpus and cannot support an AI or author-geography inference.
- Cleaning stripped URLs, mentions, punctuation and hashtag symbols while retaining surviving lexical text. Consequently, exact promotional-link and hashtag counts cannot be reconstructed reliably from processed text.
- Repeated wording, templates, promotional vocabulary, links, hashtags or concentrated posting can describe corpus patterns, but do not by themselves establish automation or coordination.
- c-TF-IDF terms and representative excerpts can overstate signals from prolific accounts. The audit deliberately sampled across authors; the original evidence tables remain available for comparison.
- The displayed topic, sentiment and emotion correspondences are author-level aggregates computed before this review. They were not label inputs and should not be read as validation of a label.
- Omega-squared is a bias-corrected association measure, not a causal estimate and not a measure of label purity.
- The visualiser's Example post is deterministically content-rich, not a statistically representative post for that author or community.
